The audience most likely to recognise the problem and least likely to care what

---

AI has become very good at producing work. I am less convinced that a chat
window is always the best place for a human to think about that work.

The last few months I noticed a pattern in myself. I would ask for a strategy
memo, get something genuinely good in twenty seconds, skim it in the same
window I asked for it in — and then approve something I had not really read.
The screen that produced it was not a good place to argue with it.

So I built the missing piece: my AI assistant sends the document to my Kindle
Scribe. I read it in an armchair with no notifications, cross out the
assumption I do not believe, write "too operational — frame this for the board"
in the margin, and send the pages back. The assistant reads my handwriting,
understands that the note belongs to that paragraph, and revises from there.

The part I did not expect: it reads *layout*. A strike-through means delete. An
arrow from the margin means insert here. A circled word means that word, not
the sentence. I never taught it the notation — it reads a page the way a
colleague would.

What changed for me is not speed. It is that I now disagree with AI-generated
work more often, and earlier, because I am reading it somewhere designed for
reading rather than somewhere designed for replying.

It is open source and self-hosted, MIT licensed, and works with Claude, Codex
and ChatGPT desktop. It runs on your own machine and needs no account with me:
https://github.com/starkshtlm/kindle-scribe-mcp

If you use AI for serious work and have been feeling that same friction, I would
genuinely like to hear how you handle it — pen, print, or something better than
both.
